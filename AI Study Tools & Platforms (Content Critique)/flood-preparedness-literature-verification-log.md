## 2. AI-Generated Literature Review Summary

Flood preparedness and urban drainage challenges in Davao City remain important concerns in local development planning, especially as the city continues to experience heavy rainfall, localized flooding, drainage congestion, and risks in low-lying communities. Flooding in urban areas is commonly linked to several factors, including intense rainfall, river overflow, insufficient drainage capacity, clogged canals, solid waste accumulation, and the expansion of residential and commercial areas near waterways.

In Davao City, barangay-level disaster preparedness plays a significant role in reducing flood-related risks. Barangay officials, local disaster risk reduction committees, and community volunteers are often responsible for monitoring flood-prone areas, issuing advisories, assisting vulnerable residents, and coordinating evacuation when necessary. This shows that flood preparedness is not only an infrastructure concern but also a governance and community coordination issue.

Urban drainage improvement is also a major part of flood risk reduction. Drainage systems must be regularly maintained to prevent clogging, especially in densely populated areas where improper waste disposal can block canals and waterways. However, flooding should not be explained by waste disposal alone, since rainfall intensity, land use, river conditions, road elevation, and drainage design also affect flood exposure.

Overall, the literature suggests that Davao City’s flood preparedness requires both structural and non-structural solutions. Structural responses include drainage upgrades, flood-control systems, and river management. Non-structural responses include early warning systems, public advisories, evacuation planning, community education, and stronger barangay coordination. A balanced approach is necessary to protect residents, businesses, and vulnerable groups from recurring flood risks.

## 5. Human Corrections and Empirical Notes

The AI-generated literature review was generally useful because it organized the topic into major themes such as rainfall, drainage, solid waste, infrastructure, and barangay coordination. However, manual checking was still necessary because AI can make claims sound certain even when they need stronger evidence.

The first correction is that flooding in Davao City should not be explained through only one cause. Some AI-generated outputs may overemphasize solid waste disposal, but verified sources show that flooding is also connected to heavy rainfall, low-lying areas, clogged drainage, lack of storm drains, drainage capacity, and infrastructure limitations.

The second correction is that AI should avoid claiming that flood-control projects have already solved the city’s flooding problem. Available sources show that flood-control and drainage planning are continuing priorities. Reports about 265 flood-prone or severely affected areas indicate that flooding remains a concern.

The third correction is that community preparedness should not be treated as separate from infrastructure. In Davao City, flood risk reduction requires both physical projects, such as drainage systems and flood-control structures, and community-based actions, such as advisories, evacuation planning, barangay coordination, and public awareness.

## 6. Critical Reflection on AI Tool Limitations

The AI tool was useful for quickly producing a structured summary of the issue. It helped identify important themes such as flood-prone barangays, urban drainage, solid waste, heavy rainfall, disaster preparedness, and barangay-level coordination.

However, the AI output still needed human review. One limitation is that AI can produce broad claims without giving exact evidence. Another limitation is that AI can make a statement sound verified even when it is only a reasonable assumption. For example, the AI may say that Davao City has “made progress” without clearly identifying which project, report, or government action proves that progress.

AI can also oversimplify complex problems. Flooding may be described as a result of improper waste disposal alone, but verified sources show that the issue is more complex. Rainfall intensity, drainage capacity, low-lying geography, river overflow, lack of storm drains, urban development, and public preparedness all contribute to flood risk.

Because of these limitations, AI-generated research summaries should not be submitted directly as final academic or policy work. They must be checked against official reports, government pages, disaster monitoring documents, and credible news sources before being used in a research brief.

## 7. Final Verified Summary

Flood preparedness and urban drainage remain important development priorities in Davao City. Verified sources show that flooding is connected to heavy rainfall, low-lying barangays, river overflow, clogged drainage, lack of storm drains, and the need for long-term flood-control and drainage planning.

Reports from DSWD DROMIC confirm that flooding incidents have affected low-lying and flood-prone areas in Davao City, including parts of Matina. Reports from local news sources also show that 265 areas in Davao City have been identified as flood-prone or severely affected by street flooding. These findings show that flooding remains a continuing concern and has not been fully eliminated.

The evidence also shows that flood risk reduction requires both infrastructure and community preparedness. Drainage upgrades, flood-control structures, retention systems, and storm drains are important, but barangay-level coordination, public advisories, evacuation planning, responder deployment, and community awareness are also necessary.

Therefore, the corrected conclusion is that Davao City’s flood preparedness challenge is not only an engineering problem. It is also a governance, planning, and community coordination issue that requires long-term action from the LGU, barangays, disaster response offices, and residents.
